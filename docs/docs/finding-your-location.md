# Finding your location

To show you the right timings, FindStarlink needs to know where you'll be watching from. There are three ways to set your location.

## Search by name

1. Pick your **Country** from the dropdown.
2. Start typing your place in the **Place** box. After the first 2 characters, up to 5 matching places appear - tap one to use it.
3. If nothing is found, you'll see *"Sorry, nothing found. Please check the name!"* along with an option to *"put your coordinates"* instead.

Your recently used places are remembered - focus the Place box to see a short **Recent** list and pick one with a single tap.

### Why isn't my city listed?

To keep the download size of the city list manageable, only places with a population over 15,000 are included - including every city in the world would make the site massive to download, and it wouldn't load well in remote/rural locations.

Not to worry: you can select any city within 80 km/50 miles of you, and the timing predictions will be the same within 80 km/50 miles of a location. If your place isn't listed, you can also enter your [coordinates](#search-by-coordinates) directly.

## Search by coordinates

Switch to the **By coordinates** tab and enter your position as degrees, choosing **North** or **South** for latitude and **East** or **West** for longitude. This is handy if your place isn't in the list, or if you're somewhere remote (a dark field is the best place to watch, after all!).

## Auto-detect my location

If you haven't searched for a place yet, FindStarlink tries to locate you automatically when you first open it, using a quick lookup of your approximate location from your IP address.

=== "Web"

    On your first visit, your location is guessed from your IP address. If that isn't enough, the site then asks your browser for your precise location.

=== "Android"

    Your location is guessed from your IP address, and the app then uses your device's location to fine-tune it.

=== "iOS"

    Your location is guessed from your IP address, and the app then uses your device's location to fine-tune it.

When this asks for permission, allowing it fills in your location automatically - you can always change it afterwards by searching or entering coordinates.
